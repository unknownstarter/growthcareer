TOPIK (the Test of Proficiency in Korean) measures the Korean ability of people whose first language is not Korean. It is often required for study, work, and residence purposes. The details below are for 2026. Dates and fees can change, so always confirm the latest information on [topik.go.kr](https://www.topik.go.kr) before you register.

## 2026 Test Schedule

In 2026 there are 6 paper-based tests (PBT), 6 internet-based tests (IBT), and 3 speaking tests. PBT is held on Sundays and IBT on Saturdays. The domestic test dates are below.

| Round | PBT (Sunday) | IBT (Saturday) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | January 11 | February 28 |
| 2 | April 12 | March 21 |
| 3 | May 17 | June 13 |
| 4 | July 5 | September 12 |
| 5 | October 18 | October 24 |
| 6 | November 15 | November 28 |

> If you register from outside Korea, the registration period and test date may differ from the domestic schedule. Overseas test takers should confirm separately with their local test center.

## PBT vs IBT: What Is the Difference

PBT (paper-based test) means you write your answers on a paper booklet, while IBT (internet-based test) is taken on a computer. Both determine your level, but they are commonly reported to differ in how long the results take to come out. IBT results generally come out faster than PBT, so some people choose IBT when their study or visa schedule is tight.

For the exact result release timing and any differences between formats, check the round-by-round notices on [topik.go.kr](https://www.topik.go.kr), since they can vary by round.

## How to Register

TOPIK registration is done online. The general flow is as follows.

- Sign up and log in at topik.go.kr
- Choose the round and level (TOPIK I or TOPIK II)
- Confirm your personal details
- Upload a compliant ID photo
- Pay the fee
- Confirm the "registration complete" status

Registration opens at a specific time listed in each round's notice, and popular test centers can fill up quickly. It helps to check the opening time and remaining spots in the notice ahead of time.

> The ID photo is commonly reported to require a 3x4cm white-background photo (JPG) taken within the last 6 months, but confirm the exact photo requirements in the round notice.

## Levels and Cutoffs

The levels you can earn depend on which test you take. TOPIK I determines levels 1 and 2 out of a total of 200 points, while TOPIK II determines levels 3 through 6 out of a total of 300 points.

The score cutoffs for each level are as follows.

| Test | Level | Score Range |
|---|---|---|
| TOPIK I | Level 1 | 80 to 139 |
| TOPIK I | Level 2 | 140 to 200 |
| TOPIK II | Level 3 | 120 to 149 |
| TOPIK II | Level 4 | 150 to 189 |
| TOPIK II | Level 5 | 190 to 229 |
| TOPIK II | Level 6 | 230 to 300 |

## Where Your Level Is Used

TOPIK levels are required for university applications, employment, residence status, and more. Level 3 or 4 and above is commonly reported to be required for university study or work visas, but the required level depends on the institution and purpose.

For exact requirements, check directly with the university you are applying to, the employer you are joining, or the level guidance on [topik.go.kr](https://www.topik.go.kr).

## Domestic Fees

The 2026 domestic fees are as follows.

| Test Type | Fee |
|---|---|
| PBT TOPIK I | 40,000 won |
| PBT TOPIK II | 55,000 won |
| IBT TOPIK I | 70,000 won |
| IBT TOPIK II | 95,000 won |
| Speaking Test | 80,000 won |

## What to Know on Test Day

You must bring your original ID on test day. Copies and photos are not accepted. Check the night before that you have your ID and that you know the test center location and entry time.
